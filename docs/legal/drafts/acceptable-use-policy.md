# Acceptable Use Policy — DRAFT

> **Draft for the owner (ruling 13). Not published, not reviewed by a lawyer.**
> It takes over the rules in Terms of Service §4 so there is one list, not two.
> Every rule below describes something the platform already does or already
> forbids in the published Terms; nothing new is promised that the software does
> not do. Changes from Terms §4 are noted at the end.

**Effective:** [OWNER: set in the Legal Center editor]
**Applies to:** everybody who uses WeVolunteer — students, organizers, club
managers, partners and administrators.

These are the rules for using WeVolunteer. They are part of the
[Terms of Service](/legal/terms-of-service): breaking them is breaking the Terms.
The [Community Guidelines](/legal/community-guidelines) explain what they look
like in practice.

## 1. Towards other people

Do not:

- harass, bully, threaten or intimidate anyone;
- post or send anything sexual involving a minor, ever, in any form;
- comment on anybody's body or appearance, or ask anybody for photos;
- ask a student to keep a conversation secret from their parents, guardians or
  teachers;
- ask a student for private contact details — phone number, home address,
  social media handles;
- offer anybody money, gifts, alcohol, vapes or drugs;
- ask to meet a student alone, anywhere, for any reason.

## 2. With information

Do not:

- lie about your identity, age, grade, school or role, or impersonate anybody;
- share another person's private information — contact details, address, photo
  or schedule — without their permission;
- use a student's contact details for anything other than the event you received
  them for;
- use certificate verification to find out whether a particular person
  volunteers, to collect students' names, or to try codes at scale.

## 3. With the service itself

Do not:

- claim volunteer hours you did not work, or help anybody else do so;
- create more than one account, or an account to get around a block or a
  suspension;
- try to influence an Auto Select draw, a leaderboard position or an hours total
  by any means other than volunteering;
- file reports you know to be false, or use reporting or appeals to harass
  somebody — including sending appeal after appeal to a club that has said no;
- scrape or bulk-download WeVolunteer, or resell access to it (section 4 says
  what automation is fine);
- get around a limit rather than working within it (section 5);
- deliberately disrupt the service or make it worse for other people (section 6);
- exploit a bug instead of reporting it (section 7);
- upload or send malware, or anything designed to damage a device or take
  control of one;
- try to break, probe or get around the security of the service, or reach an
  account, event or record you were not given — except as section 7 allows;
- use WeVolunteer to advertise, recruit, sell or fundraise for anything that is
  not a volunteer event;
- post anything illegal.

## 4. Automation

**A commitment to turn up has to be made by a person.** Do not run anything that
applies for a place, accepts one or cancels one without you seeing it and
deciding. Auto Select is a random draw held after the deadline, so applying
faster wins nothing — the rule exists because an application is a promise that a
real person will be somewhere, and a script cannot make one.

Fine: reading your own information however you like; putting your commitments
into your own calendar automatically; watching for opportunities and telling
yourself about them; assistive technology of any kind, including tools that fill
in or navigate for you.

Not fine: applying, accepting or cancelling without a person deciding each one;
holding places you do not mean to take; running enough requests to affect anybody
else's use of the service.

Not sure which side something falls on? Ask at **support@wevolunteers.org**
before you run it.

## 5. Limits

WeVolunteer has limits so that one account cannot crowd out everybody else:
how often you can try to sign in, how many requests you can make in a short
time, how many opportunities you can have a pending application to, how many
problem reports and appeals you can send. They are published rather than hidden
so that tools can respect them.

If you hit a limit, wait, or tell us it is wrong. Do not route around it — with
more accounts, more devices, more addresses or anything else.

## 6. Disruption

Flooding WeVolunteer with requests, filling it with content nobody will use, or
setting out to slow it down or take it offline is forbidden **whether or not it
works**. That includes attacks from many machines at once (denial of service).
We may block the source of such traffic — an account, a device or a network —
without warning, and that can affect other people on the same network. We will
lift a block that turns out to have caught the wrong people as soon as we know.

## 7. Security problems

If you think you have found a security or privacy flaw, email
**security@wevolunteers.org** with enough detail to reproduce it, and say
"security" in the subject line.

If you report in good faith, use only your own account and data, and do not
break the service to prove it can be broken — no load testing, no denial of
service, no changing data that is not yours — we will not treat it as a breach
of these rules and will not pursue you for it. Give us a reasonable chance to fix
it before telling anybody else. There is no payment; we will credit you if you
would like.

## 8. What happens if these rules are broken

Depending on how serious it is: a warning; removal of what was posted;
suspension of the account for a time or until lifted; or, for the most serious
— anything that puts a student at risk — closing the account and, where the law
requires or a student's safety needs it, telling the people who must know.

A suspended person can still sign in to read why and to appeal, and is told how.
Suspension does not erase anybody's volunteer record.

## 9. Changes

We may change this policy. How we tell you, and how much notice you get, is set
out in Terms of Service §13.
