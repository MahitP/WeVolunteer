# 14. Acceptable Use Policy

## Specification

**Purpose.** One list of the rules for using WeVolunteer, taken over from Terms of Service §4,
§4.1 and §4.2 so the rules are written once and the Terms point here.

**Audience.** Everybody who uses WeVolunteer: students, organizers, club managers, partners and
administrators.

**Consent type.** `notice` / `notice`. It is part of the Terms of Service, which every account
accepts, so it carries no acceptance of its own. Terms 2.0 (published through the Legal Center
editor, effective 2026-11-06) replaces §4 with a pointer here; this document takes effect the
same day so the two never disagree.

**Decisions.** Ruling 13 (2026-09-30): a separate Acceptable Use Policy. Owner, 2026-10-05:
publish; the limits section says "we tell you a limit when you reach it", which is what the
product does, rather than promising a published list of values.

**Changes from Terms §4.** Sections 1–4 and 7 carry every rule and promise in §4, §4.1 and
§4.2, shortened where the shorter wording means the same; the approved draft had dropped six of
them (the accidental-data rule, "even though the software let you", coordinating with an
organizer, "while you are asleep", fixing the rule for assistive technology, and what happens to
a report), and they were restored before publication so that moving the rules changes none of
them. Added: appeal spam (section 3, FX-70), limits (section 5), denial of service (section 6),
enforcement (section 8). Removed: §4.2's "for a school district" (Posture B), and its sentence
that limits are published (the owner chose "we tell you a limit when you reach it").

## Draft

# Acceptable Use Policy

**Version 1.0 · Takes effect 2026-11-06**

These are the rules for using WeVolunteer. They apply to everybody who uses it — students,
organizers, club managers, partners and administrators. They are part of the
[Terms of Service](/legal/terms-of-service): breaking them is breaking the Terms. The
[Community Guidelines](/legal/community-guidelines) explain what they look like in practice.

## 1. Toward other people

Do not:

- harass, bully, threaten or intimidate anyone;
- post or send anything sexual involving a minor, ever, in any form;
- comment on anybody's body or appearance, or ask anybody for photos;
- ask a student to keep a conversation secret from their parents, guardians or teachers;
- ask a student for private contact details — phone number, home address, social media
  handles;
- offer anybody money, gifts, alcohol, vapes or drugs;
- ask to meet a student alone, anywhere, for any reason.

## 2. With information

Do not:

- lie about your identity, age, grade, school or role, or impersonate anybody;
- share another person's private information — contact details, address, photo or schedule —
  without their permission;
- use a student's contact details for anything other than the event you received them for;
- use certificate verification to find out whether a particular person volunteers, to collect
  students' names, or to try codes at scale.

## 3. With the service itself

Do not:

- claim volunteer hours you did not work, or help anybody else do so;
- create more than one account, or an account to get around a block or a suspension;
- try to influence an Auto Select draw, a leaderboard position or an hours total by any means
  other than volunteering — including coordinating with an organizer to record hours that were
  not worked;
- file reports you know to be false, or use reporting or appeals to harass somebody —
  including sending appeal after appeal to a club that has said no;
- scrape or bulk-download WeVolunteer, or resell access to it (section 4 says what automation
  is fine);
- get around a limit rather than working within it (section 5);
- deliberately disrupt the service or make it worse for other people (section 6);
- exploit a bug instead of reporting it. If something obviously unintended works in your
  favor, using it is breaking these rules even though the software let you; section 7 is how to
  report it;
- upload or send malware, or anything designed to damage a device or take control of one;
- try to break, probe or get around the security of the service, or reach an account, event or
  record you were not given — except as section 7 allows;
- use WeVolunteer to advertise, recruit, sell or fundraise for anything that is not a volunteer
  event;
- post anything illegal.

## 4. Automation

**A commitment to turn up has to be made by a person.** Do not run anything that applies for a
place, accepts one or cancels one without you seeing it and deciding. Auto Select is a random
draw held after the deadline, so applying faster wins nothing — the rule exists because an
application is a promise that a real person will be somewhere, and a script cannot make one.

Fine: reading your own information however you like; putting your commitments into your own
calendar automatically; watching for opportunities and telling yourself about them; assistive
technology of any kind, including tools that fill in or navigate for you. Nothing in this
section is meant to get in the way of assistive technology, and if it does, tell us and we will
fix the rule.

Not fine: applying, accepting or cancelling without a person deciding each one, including while
you are asleep, at school or otherwise not looking; holding places you do not mean to take;
running enough requests to affect anybody else's use of the service.

Not sure which side something falls on? Ask at **support@wevolunteers.org** before you run it.

## 5. Limits

WeVolunteer has limits so that one account cannot crowd out everybody else: how often you can
try to sign in, how many requests you can make in a short time, how many opportunities you can
have a pending application to, how many problem reports and appeals you can send. When you reach
one, we tell you.

If you hit a limit, wait, or tell us it is wrong. Do not route around it — with more accounts,
more devices, more addresses or anything else.

## 6. Disruption

Flooding WeVolunteer with requests, filling it with content nobody will use, or setting out to
slow it down or take it offline is forbidden **whether or not it works**. That includes attacks
from many machines at once (denial of service). We may block the source of such traffic — an
account, a device or a network — without warning, and that can affect other people on the same
network. We will lift a block that turns out to have caught the wrong people as soon as we know.

## 7. Security problems

If you think you have found a security or privacy flaw, email **security@wevolunteers.org** with
enough detail to reproduce it. That address is also the safety inbox, so say "security" in the
subject line and it will be read as a technical report.

If you report in good faith and follow these two rules, we will not treat it as a breach of these
rules and will not pursue you for it:

1. **Use your own account and your own data.** Do not read, change or keep anybody else's
   information — and if you come across somebody else's data by accident, stop, and tell us what
   you saw so we know what was exposed.
2. **Do not break the service to prove it can be broken.** No load testing, no denial of
   service, no deleting or changing data that is not yours.

Give us a reasonable chance to fix it before telling anybody else. We will confirm we got your
report, tell you what we found, and say when it is fixed. There is no payment; we will credit you
if you would like.

## 8. What happens if these rules are broken

Depending on how serious it is: a warning; removal of what was posted; suspension of the account
for a time or until lifted; or, for the most serious — anything that puts a student at risk —
closing the account and, where the law requires or a student's safety needs it, telling the
people who must know.

A suspended person can still sign in to read why and to appeal, and is told how. Suspension does
not erase anybody's volunteer record.

## 9. Changes

We may change this policy. How we tell you, and how much notice you get, is set out in
[Terms of Service §13](/legal/terms-of-service).
