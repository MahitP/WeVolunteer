# 3. Community Guidelines

## Specification

**Purpose.** Give the enforceable behavioural rules a concrete, readable form, so that
moderation decisions rest on a published standard rather than an administrator's mood.

**Audience.** Students and organizers, at the moment they are about to post.

**Required sections.** The principle · what belongs here · what does not · messages ·
comments · profiles · hours honesty · reporting · what we do about violations · appeals.

**Decisions resolved.** The moderation SLA is the one published below. **Appeals go to a
WeVolunteer administrator** — there is no district and no external appeal body, and the draft
says so rather than implying an independence that does not exist.

**Guardian notification removed.** This document previously promised to tell a parent or guardian
about a suspension or removal for any user under 18. **WeVolunteer collects no guardian contact
details and no mechanism does**, so that promise could not be kept for anyone. It has been
replaced with what actually happens. Do not reintroduce it without the collection mechanism to
back it, and per the current product decision that mechanism is not being built.

**Why this matters legally.** Content moderation without a published standard is arbitrary,
and arbitrary enforcement against a student is the kind of thing a parent escalates. A published
ladder of consequences is also the evidence of good faith if a harassment or bullying complaint
ever turns into a claim — and because WeVolunteer is independent, there is no district behind it
to absorb that. This document is the standard.

## Draft

# Community Guidelines

**Version 2.1 · Last updated 2026-08-05** · Part of the
[Terms of Service](02-terms-of-service.md), and accepted with them

WeVolunteer is a small community of students and the adults who organise volunteer work. It
works because people are decent to each other. These are the rules that keep it that way.

**They apply everywhere you can type:** messages, event comments, your profile, event
descriptions.

## The principle

Assume the person reading is a fourteen-year-old you will see at school on Monday. Write like
that.

## What belongs here

- Questions about events, shifts, and what to bring
- Coordinating who is doing what
- Thanking people, and celebrating what got done
- Telling an organizer you cannot make it
- Telling us when something is wrong

## What does not

### Never, and we will not warn you first

- **Anything sexual involving a minor**, in any form
- **Threats of violence**, against anyone
- **Sexual harassment**, including comments about someone's body or appearance
- **Grooming behaviour** — asking a student for private contact details, asking them to keep
  secrets, or offering gifts
- **Sharing someone's private information** — address, phone number, schedule, photos —
  without their permission
- **Content that sexualises anyone**

Accounts doing these things are removed, and where a student may have been harmed we report
it. See the [Youth Safety Guidelines](08-youth-safety-guidelines.md).

### Not allowed, and we will usually warn first

- Bullying, name-calling, or piling on
- Excluding or targeting someone because of their race, religion, disability, gender,
  sexuality, or where they are from
- Spam, advertising, or recruiting for anything unrelated to volunteering
- Lying about who you are
- Starting arguments in event comments
- Claiming hours you did not work
- Repeatedly signing up and not turning up

## Messages

- Keep conversations on WeVolunteer. If somebody wants to move to text, Snapchat, or
  Instagram, that is worth reporting on its own.
- Adults message students **about events only**.
- Nobody should ask you to keep a conversation secret.
- Every conversation has a Report button. Use it.

## Comments on events

Comments are seen by everyone at that event, including the organizer. Ask your questions here
— usually somebody else has the same one.

Do not use comments to argue with the organizer's decisions. If you were not selected, message
them, or ask us.

## Your profile

Your bio and skills are about volunteering. Do not put your phone number, home address,
social media handles, or anything else you would not want an adult stranger to have.

**Your profile is private by default.** If you make it public, other signed-in users can see
it. That is your choice to make — just make it knowingly.

## Being honest about hours

Volunteer hours count toward real things. Faking them takes something from students who did
the work.

- Do not ask an organizer to credit hours you did not work.
- Do not sign in for someone who is not there.
- If your hours look wrong, tell the organizer. Mistakes happen and get fixed.

Organizers who credit hours that were not worked lose their accounts.

## Reporting

Use the **Report** button on any conversation, comment, or event. Or email
**wevolunteer.platform@gmail.com**.

**We never tell the person you reported who reported them.** Your name is attached to the
report so we can ask you a follow-up question — an administrator can see it, and nobody else
can. If you would rather we did not know either, email the address above from an account that
is not yours, or call the hotlines in the Youth Safety Guidelines, which take anonymous
reports.

You will not get in trouble for reporting something that turns out to be nothing — we would
far rather look.

## What we do about violations

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1 | We remove the content and tell you why |
| 2 | A warning on your account |
| 3 | Suspension — you cannot sign in, for a stated period |
| 4 | Your account is removed |

We skip straight to step 4 for anything in the "never" list.

**Our response times:** a report reaches a human within **one business day**. Anything flagged
as a safety concern is looked at **the same day**.

**We tell you** — the account holder — what happened and why, at the email address on your
account. **We do not automatically tell your parent or guardian**, because we do not ask for
their contact details and do not have them. The exception is a situation where someone may have
been harmed: then we go to the authorities, and to your school or a guardian if we can reach
them.

## If you think we got it wrong

Email **wevolunteer.platform@gmail.com** and say what happened and why you disagree. We reply
within **5 business days** with the outcome and the reason. If we were wrong, we say so and
undo it.

> **Honest note, and it stays in the published version:** WeVolunteer is a small team. We
> cannot always promise that a different person reviews your appeal than the one who made the
> original decision. We will tell you who reviewed it. We would rather say that than promise an
> independence we cannot deliver.
